The internet is very good at asking for our attention. It is less practiced at
giving that attention somewhere worthwhile to rest.

I have been thinking about what it means to make a calmer digital place—not an
empty or austere one, but a place with enough breathing room for curiosity.

## Calm is not the absence of character

Quiet interfaces can still be expressive. Type can have a point of view.
Movement can clarify. Color can carry warmth. The distinction is whether those
choices support the idea or compete with it.

The smallest useful test I know is to ask: **what is this element helping
someone understand?** If the answer is unclear, the design may be asking for
attention it has not earned.

## Leave a little space unfinished

Not every edge needs filling. Not every visit needs converting. A thoughtful
pause, a clear sentence, or a page that simply ends can be a feature.

I want to make more things that feel complete without feeling crowded—places
that respect the person on the other side of the screen.
