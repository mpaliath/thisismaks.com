# A Brief Introduction to Me

If you had met me ten years ago, you would probably have guessed my life would revolve around software.

You would have been right.

What you probably wouldn't have guessed is *why* I enjoy building software.

It has never really been about the code.

I've always been fascinated by systems—understanding how complicated things work, why they fail, and how small changes can create surprisingly large improvements. Software just happens to be one of the best mediums for exploring those ideas.

That curiosity led me through more than sixteen years at Microsoft, and today I work as a Staff Software Engineer at Walmart, where I spend my days solving large-scale engineering problems. I genuinely enjoy untangling complexity. The harder the problem, the more interesting it becomes.

But somewhere along the way, I noticed something.

The most meaningful problems in life aren't technical.

They're human.

How do you raise children who are curious instead of merely successful?

How do you build products that people actually need instead of products that are merely impressive?

How do you become healthier at fifty than you were at forty?

How do you invest rationally when everyone else is emotional?

How do you build a business without losing sight of your family?

Those questions don't have compilers or unit tests.

They're messy.

And that's exactly why they fascinate me.

Outside of work, I'm a husband, a father of two energetic boys, and an eternal student. My children have probably taught me more about learning than any engineering textbook ever did. Watching them discover the world reminds me that curiosity is something to protect, not outgrow.

That same mindset spills into almost everything I do.

One week I might be researching the psychology behind recurring conflicts in relationships. The next, I'm comparing investment strategies used by great value investors. Then I'm designing a survey platform, experimenting with AI-powered educational products, or figuring out how yoga principles can be translated into technology that helps people live healthier lives.

Some people collect hobbies.

I seem to collect questions.

I don't believe knowledge lives in isolated boxes. Engineering has taught me lessons that apply to parenting. Investing has made me a better product builder. Psychology has influenced how I lead teams. Even conversations with my children have changed how I think about artificial intelligence.

Everything connects.

This blog is where I explore those connections.

You won't find a single niche here.

Instead, you'll find thoughtful essays about software engineering, artificial intelligence, startups, investing, parenting, health, leadership, productivity, and whatever else captures my attention long enough for me to ask, "Why does this work?"

I'm not writing because I have all the answers.

Quite the opposite.

I'm writing because putting ideas into words is one of the best ways I've found to challenge my own assumptions. If something survives careful thinking and honest discussion, it's probably worth keeping. If it doesn't, I'm happy to replace it with a better idea.

My hope is that some of these explorations will save you time, help you see a familiar problem differently, or simply give you something interesting to think about on your morning coffee.

If that happens, then this blog has done its job.

Welcome.
