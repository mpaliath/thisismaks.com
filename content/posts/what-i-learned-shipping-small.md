Small projects create a useful kind of honesty. There is less room to hide the
essential idea behind a roadmap, a rewrite, or one more round of preparation.

Shipping something small asks a direct question: **does this help anyone yet?**

## A finished loop teaches more

An idea becomes much more concrete after it travels the full distance from a
rough note to something another person can use. Along that route you meet the
details that planning alone cannot reveal: the confusing sentence, the missing
state, the assumption that only made sense in your own head.

Keeping the first version narrow makes it possible to complete that loop while
the original curiosity is still alive.

## Small does not mean careless

Scope and craft are different variables. A tiny thing can be carefully made.
In fact, fewer parts can make thoughtful decisions more visible.

The goal is not to rush. It is to choose a small enough promise that you can
keep it, learn from it, and decide what deserves to come next.
